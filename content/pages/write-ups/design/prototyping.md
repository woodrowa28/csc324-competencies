# Prototyping


What design questions did your intend to answer with your prototype(s)?

> Could you tell what you needed to do?

> Do the starting mechanisms feel appropriate?

> Is the initial story compelling and logical?

>  Does the medium of the story match with the mechanisms?

How did you design your prototype(s) and subsequent user studies to address these questions?

> We made our prototype a theoretical tutorial of our game, with the main production centered around papyrus --> paper instead of wood --> paper. This allowed us to mock up the start of the game, as the introduction of idle mechanics was something we wanted to focus on. We created movable tabs, sliders, and a "cursor" for users to interact with in order to give the users as accurate of an experience as possible. I sat in front of them, growing the plants and moving the counters as needed. This allowed us to mimic the separation of game execution from player interaction, allowing us to examine how the user would react to the prompts they were given. Our experimental playbook gave a script of lines to say at specific times

What answers to your design questions did your efforts unveil?

> **Could you tell what you needed to do?** Yes, players could generally tell what the next step was. The paper pop-ups we added (text boxes that would appear upon a trigger and disappear once the desired action had been performed) provided enough guidance for players to not be overwhelmed by the interface. It also helped a lot that not everything was visible at once, and advanced things like the meeple only appeared later. Players did report lacking motivation for/understanding of why they should be completing these tasks, though.

> **Do the starting mechanisms feel appropriate?** Mixed results. While this setup felt natural for the start of the game, players noted that having to complete a tutorial like this and then immediately transitioning into the full game would feel a bit like restarting and completing the same intro process twice. We should re-evaluate whether a tutorial fits.

> **Is the initial story compelling and logical?** Somewhat. The players seemed intrigued by the premise, with one even laughing aloud at the story introduction. Once it became clear that they would not get more story details, however, some players began to feel a little aimless; this feeling was strongest in those who did not have previous experience in the idle genre, supporting the idea that our "extra" features are critical to getting new players interested.

>  **Does the medium of the story match with the mechanisms?** Users did not have much feedback for this one, but it seems like yes. The story revolving around a paper god fits well with the gameplay increasing paper production.
